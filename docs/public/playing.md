# Playing

Choose a game on the front page. The player downloads what it needs, starts
the game, and shows it with the N-Gage's keys mapped to your keyboard, your
gamepad or the touch screen.

The first start of a game downloads it. After that it is kept on your device
and starts without downloading again.

## What you need

A current desktop browser: Chrome, Edge or Firefox. If something required is
missing, the player says what when it opens.

Phones and tablets get on-screen controls, but they have not been tested on
real devices and will be slower than a computer. Safari has not been tested.

Sound starts after your first click, tap or key press, because browsers do not
let a page play sound before that.

## Controls

The N-Gage has a direction pad that also presses in (OK), two soft keys under
the screen, a phone keypad and a Clear key. Most games use 5 and 7 as their
main action keys.

Many games have a layout of their own, and the player tells you what each key
does in that game: open **Menu → Guide**. The tables below are the standard
layout, used when a game has none.

### Keyboard

| N-Gage key | Keyboard |
|---|---|
| Direction pad | Arrow keys |
| OK | Enter or Space |
| Left soft key | Q |
| Right soft key | W |
| 0 to 9 | The digit row or the numeric keypad |
| ✱ | - (minus), or \* on the numeric keypad |
| # | =, or / on the numeric keypad |
| Clear | Backspace |

### Gamepad

A gamepad is picked up when you press a button on it.

| N-Gage key | Gamepad |
|---|---|
| Direction pad | D-pad or left stick |
| OK | A |
| Clear | B |
| 5 | X |
| 7 | Y |
| Left soft key | LB or Start |
| Right soft key | RB or Back |
| ✱ | LT |
| # | RT |

Button names are the Xbox ones; on other pads it is the button in the same
position.

### Touch

On a phone or tablet the N-Gage's keys are drawn around the screen: the
direction pad and the left soft key on the left, the keypad and the right soft
key on the right.

### Changing the keys

**Menu → Controls** lists every N-Gage key with the keys and buttons bound to
it. Add a binding with **+**, remove one by clicking it, and go back to the
defaults with **Reset this layout**. Your changes are remembered in this
browser.

## The menu

**Pause** and **Menu** are above the picture. The game also pauses by itself
when you switch to another tab, except while you are playing with someone
else.

The menu restarts the game, mutes the sound, goes fullscreen, and opens the
controls, the display settings, your saves, multiplayer and the guide to the
running game.
**Copy link to this game** gives you a link that starts it directly, and
**All games** takes you back to the front page.

## Saves

Save from inside the game, with the game's own menu, as you would on the
phone. Saves are kept in this browser, on this device.

**Menu → Saves** can export all your saves as one file and import them again.
Use it as a backup, or to move your saves to another browser or device.

Clearing this site's data in the browser deletes your saves and the games kept
on your device, and a private window keeps nothing after it closes. Export
first.

## Playing with someone else

Many N-Gage games could be played together over Bluetooth: one phone hosted a
game and the others joined it. Here you can do the same over the internet.
Each of you plays in your own browser, and to the games the other players
look like N-Gages nearby.

First meet in a room:

1. Open **Menu → Multiplayer…** once the game has started.
2. Set a name. It is what the other players see.
3. Choose **Host a room**. You get a room code and an invite link, with
   **Copy link** to copy it.
4. Send the link to your friend. If you would like anyone to be able to join,
   tick **List this room publicly**: the room is then shown, with your name,
   to everyone who looks at the game.

To join, your friend opens the link. Or they start the same game, open
**Menu → Multiplayer…**, choose **Join** and type the code, or pick the room
from the open rooms listed there. Open rooms are also shown on the front
page.

Then use the game's own multiplayer menu, as you would on two N-Gages: one of
you hosts the game and the others join it.

**Leave room** ends it. A room exists only while someone is in it.

Good to know:

- **Keep the tab in view while you play.** Your game keeps running when you
  switch to another tab or window, so the other player is not left waiting,
  but you do not see what happens in it meanwhile.
- **Play with two for now.** A room takes more people, but games are not
  expected to connect more than two players yet.
- **N-Gage Arena does not work.** It was Nokia's online service, and it has
  been shut down.
- **This is new, and most games have not been tried this way.** Each game's
  page says whether its multiplayer has been tried, and a game that has not
  been tried may not connect.
- While you are in a room, what your game sends to the other players passes
  through the site on its way to them, together with the name you set.

## Display

**Menu → Display** changes how the small N-Gage picture is enlarged: sharp
pixels (the default), smooth, or one of several filters, grouped by what they
suit. For 2D games, which most N-Gage games are, **xBRZ** and **MMPX** redraw
pixel art with smoother edges; for 3D games **FSR** is the one to try. These
change only the look, not the speed of the game.

For pixels that are all the same size, set **Resolution** to **Largest whole
multiple** (it leaves a margin), or keep the picture large and choose
**Sharp pixels, evened**.

The N-Gage's screen is taller than it is wide, so on a monitor there is empty
space beside the picture. **Fill the screen (beta)** uses it: **Stretch**
widens the picture over the whole area, and **Ambient fill** keeps the
picture's shape and fills the space beside it with a soft, blurred copy of
what is on screen. Neither makes the game show more of its world: a game
always draws the same picture.

## Playing your own copy

The player can also run a game from files of your own. Open **Menu → Load a
game…** and give it a dump of a game card, either as a file from your device
or as a web address. It asks for a firmware dump of an N-Gage too if it has
none yet. Files you choose from your device are not uploaded anywhere.

## When something goes wrong

- **The player says the browser cannot run the emulator.** It names what is
  missing. Use a current Chrome, Edge or Firefox.
- **No sound.** Click the page or press a key once, and check that the menu
  does not say **Unmute**.
- **The game is slow or stutters.** Close other heavy tabs. The speed depends
  on your processor; the display options make no difference.
- **The other player's game does not find yours.** Check in
  **Menu → Multiplayer…** that you are both in the same room, keep both tabs
  in front, and let one game host before the other searches.
- **A game does not start.** Check its label on the front page: not every game
  works yet. **Menu → Guide** says how far the running game gets.
